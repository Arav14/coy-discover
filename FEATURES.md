# Part A: Two core features for Coy

Coy's loop today: add up to 3 crushes free (by phone or email) → they get one anonymous "someone has a crush on you" message → if they add you back, you both see the match and each other's contact. Adding is final, and invites or payments buy extra slots.

## 1. Verified Circles: add a crush without having their number

**What:** Join a "circle" by verifying a college or work email (e.g. `@yourcollege.edu.in`, `@company.com`). Within a circle, you can pick a crush from people who opted in to being findable there (name + photo, no phone needed). Coy handles the matching and the anonymous heads-up exactly as it does now.

**Why:**
- **It fixes the biggest drop-off.** Coy needs a phone number or email, but the people you have crushes on are often classmates or colleagues whose number you *don't* have. Asking around for it is exactly the exposure Coy is meant to prevent.
- **It builds on what already exists.** There's already an email-verification step. Using it to unlock a verified community makes it worth doing and raises trust ("everyone here really is from my college").
- **Growth is dense and local.** Matches only happen if *both* people use Coy, so 500 users on one campus beat 500 spread across India. Circles focus invites and the "someone has a crush on you" messages where a match is likely, so the viral loop speeds up.
- **Privacy holds.** Being findable is opt-in and can be turned off. You only see who's *in* the circle, never who added whom.

## 2. Masked first contact after a match

**What:** On a match, open a short-lived in-app chat (e.g. 72 hours) with relayed messages instead of showing phone numbers right away. Each person can choose to "share my number" (only when *both* agree), or let the chat expire. There's a one-tap report/block too.

**Why:**
- **Safety, especially for women.** Today a match gives a near-stranger your real number. A buffer means people can add crushes they only half-know without worrying about it, so more crushes get added and more matches happen.
- **It solves the awkward first message.** A match is the most valuable moment in the app, and right now it ends at "here's their number, good luck". A built-in place to say hi, with prompts, helps that turn into a conversation.
- **It keeps the payoff inside Coy.** Matches are the moment users are most likely to pay for extra slots or invite friends. Ending the experience the moment they move to WhatsApp throws that away.
- **It fits the brand.** Coy is about not exposing yourself until it's mutual. Applying the same rule to contact details is the natural next step.
